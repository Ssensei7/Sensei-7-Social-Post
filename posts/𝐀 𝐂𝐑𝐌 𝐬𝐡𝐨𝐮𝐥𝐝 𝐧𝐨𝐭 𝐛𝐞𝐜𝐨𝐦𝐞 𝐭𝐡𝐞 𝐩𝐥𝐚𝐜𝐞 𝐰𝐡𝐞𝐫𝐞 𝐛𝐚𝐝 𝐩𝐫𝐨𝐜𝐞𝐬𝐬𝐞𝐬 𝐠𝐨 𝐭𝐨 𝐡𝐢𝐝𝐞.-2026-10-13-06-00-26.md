This happens more often than people realize.

A business process is confusing.

So Salesforce gets customized to support it.

Then the process changes.

Another customization gets added.

Then an exception appears.

Another automation is created.

Eventually, Salesforce is doing a remarkable job of supporting a process that probably shouldn't exist in the first place.

The technology is working.

The business process isn't.

That's why CRM projects shouldn't begin with:

“𝑾𝒉𝒂𝒕 𝒄𝒂𝒏 𝒘𝒆 𝒃𝒖𝒊𝒍𝒅?”

Sometimes they should begin with:

“𝑾𝒉𝒚 𝒂𝒓𝒆 𝒘𝒆 𝒅𝒐𝒊𝒏𝒈 𝒕𝒉𝒊𝒔 𝒕𝒉𝒊𝒔 𝒘𝒂𝒚?”

Because automating a bad process doesn't remove the problem.

It simply makes the problem happen faster.
