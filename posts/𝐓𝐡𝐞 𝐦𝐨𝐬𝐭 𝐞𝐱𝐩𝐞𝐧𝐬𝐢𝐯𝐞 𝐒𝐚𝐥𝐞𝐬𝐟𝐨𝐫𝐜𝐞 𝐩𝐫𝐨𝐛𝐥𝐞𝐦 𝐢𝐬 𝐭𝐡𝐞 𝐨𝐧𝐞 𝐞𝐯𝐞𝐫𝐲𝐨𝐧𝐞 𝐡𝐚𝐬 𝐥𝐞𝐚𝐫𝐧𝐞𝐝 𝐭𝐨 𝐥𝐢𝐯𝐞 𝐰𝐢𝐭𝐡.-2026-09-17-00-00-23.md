It's rarely the obvious problem.

It's the five minutes someone spends doing something manually every morning.

The report someone rebuilds every Friday.

The spreadsheet someone keeps because nobody trusts the CRM data.

The information someone copies from one system into another.

The approval that still happens through email.

The workaround everyone knows about but nobody questions anymore.

Individually, these things seem small.

Multiply them across dozens or hundreds of employees, every week, for years…

They become expensive.

Not just in software costs.

In time.

In mistakes.

In delays.

In frustrated employees.

And eventually, in missed opportunities.

People stop noticing.

It becomes:

“𝑻𝒉𝒂𝒕'𝒔 𝒋𝒖𝒔𝒕 𝒉𝒐𝒘 𝒘𝒆 𝒅𝒐 𝒊𝒕.”

Sometimes the biggest opportunity in a Salesforce org isn't adding something new.

It's finally fixing the thing everyone has learned to tolerate.
